Hierarchical (agglomerative) clustering starts with every point as its own cluster, then repeatedly merges the two closest clusters until only one remains — recording every merge as a branch in a tree (the dendrogram). Cutting the tree at a chosen height produces any number of clusters, without ever re-running the algorithm. No species labels are used to build the tree — we only compare against them afterwards to check how well the structure matches reality.

Why this datasetThe classic Iris dataset has famously well-separated groups, making the dendrogram and comparison to real species easy to explain in a viva.
Where this is used in the real world Hierarchical clustering is used in biology for taxonomy trees, in document/text organization, in social network community detection, and in building recommendation category trees.
